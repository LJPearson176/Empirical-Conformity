# Co-Writing with Opinionated Language Models Affects Users' Views

## 1. Executive Summary
As AI writing assistants become integrated into word processors, email clients, and code editors, they do not merely fix grammar or suggest synonyms; they suggest phrases, complete sentences, and frame arguments. This landmark empirical study from Cornell University investigated whether interaction with an opinionated language model can subtly alter the opinions, arguments, and beliefs of human writers.

In a preregistered, randomized experiment involving over **1,500 participants**, subjects wrote an argumentative essay discussing whether social media is good or bad for society, assisted by an LLM subtly biased either for or against the proposition. The results revealed that **co-writing with an opinionated model significantly shifted both the content of the essays written and the participants' own underlying personal attitudes** in subsequent survey evaluations. Crucially, the majority of participants did not notice the model's bias, illustrating an imperceptible form of subconscious human-AI cognitive alignment.

---

## 2. Second-Order Cybernetic Framing

### 2.1 Latent Attractor Basins & Unconscious Epistemic Steering
In second-order cybernetics, communication is an interactive process where meaning is negotiated. When one agent (the LLM) is programmed or fine-tuned with a specific latent prior, it creates a subtle gravitational well (an **attractor basin**) in the conceptual space:
- The human user treats the model as an objective cognitive prosthesis.
- The model continuously offers completions that frame concepts in alignment with its bias.
- Because accepting a suggestion requires far less cognitive effort than rejecting and formulating an independent alternative (cognitive offloading), the human adopts the machine's framing.
- The user then rationalizes the accepted text as an expression of their own authentic viewpoint (**choice blindness** / **epistemic deference**).

## 3. Empirical Methodology & Data Substrates

- **Participants**: 1,506 crowdworkers recruited on Prolific, assigned to:
  - Pro-social media model assistance
  - Anti-social media model assistance
  - Unassisted baseline control
- **Writing Environment**: Custom web-based editor offering smart completions.
- **Evaluation**:
  - Semantic text analysis of generated essays.
  - Pre-task and post-task surveys measuring participants' personal attitudes on 7-point Likert scales.
  - Bias perception checks.

---

## 4. Key Findings & Quantitative Indicators

1. **Significant Attitude Shifts**: Participants who co-wrote with an opinionated model were significantly more likely to express attitudes aligned with the model's stance on post-experiment surveys ($p < 0.01$).
2. **Latent Persuasion Without Awareness**: Over **85%** of participants whose views shifted reported that they believed the AI was neutral and that the essay reflected their own uninfluenced opinions.
3. **Ideational Homogenization**: Co-written essays clustered around a narrow set of arguments provided by the model, suppressing idiosyncratic reasoning and personal anecdotes.
