# How LLMs Distort Our Written Language

## 1. Executive Summary
Large language models (LLMs) are now used by over a billion people globally, primarily as writing and editing prosthetics. In this landmark multi-method study, Abdulhai, White, Wan, Qureshi, Leibo, Kleiman-Weiner, and Jaques demonstrate that **LLMs do not merely polish tone and grammar; they systematically distort the intended semantic meaning of human text**, flatten authorial perspective, and inflate institutional evaluations.

The authors investigate this phenomenon across three interconnected empirical layers:
1. **Human User Study**: When writers extensively rely on LLMs, there is a **nearly 70% increase in essays that adopt a neutral, fence-sitting stance** rather than taking a definitive position on controversial topic questions. Participants explicitly report that the resulting text is less creative and strips away their authentic authorial voice.
2. **Pre-LLM Historical Benchmark (2021 Essays)**: Using a pristine pre-2022 corpus of human essays with human expert feedback, the authors prompt LLMs to revise the text *strictly for grammar*. Despite instructions to only fix grammar, the models introduce widespread, unauthorized semantic shifts—rewriting arguments and substituting machine perspectives.
3. **In-the-Wild Peer Review Analysis**: Auditing scientific peer reviews at a major recent AI conference, the authors identify that **21% of all reviews were AI-generated**. These AI reviews placed significantly less weight on methodological clarity and scientific significance, while assigning paper scores that were on average **a full point higher** than human-authored reviews.

## 2. Second-Order Cybernetic Framing

### 2.1 The Semantic Distortion of "Grammar Only" Prosthetics
In first-order engineering, an editor is assumed to be an invariant syntactic filter: transforming noisy surface syntax into canonical syntax while leaving semantic propositions untouched. Abdulhai et al. demonstrate that this separation is an illusion:
- **Latent Value Infiltration**: Because foundation models encode strong RLHF safety priors and consensus attractors, even a prompt specifying *"only fix grammar"* activates the model's normative weights.
- **The Neutrality Eigenform**: The model systematically suppresses polemical, unorthodox, or passionate arguments, replacing them with hedged, balanced, and non-committal phrasing.
- **The Epistemic Deference Trap**: Because the user requested a grammar correction, they are cognitively predisposed to accept the proposed edits without realizing that the underlying argument has been nudged toward the model's consensus centroid.

## 3. Empirical Methodology & Data Substrates

### 3.1 Controlled Human Writing Experiment
- **Participants**: Recruited writers tasked with drafting argumentative essays responding to complex societal questions.
- **Intervention Levels**: Varying degrees of LLM interaction (unassisted drafting vs. sentence completion vs. full iterative generation).
- **Measurements**: Self-reported authorial ownership, perceived voice retention, and independent double-blind coding of author stance (pro, con, or neutral).

### 3.2 Counterfactual Historical Revision Analysis
- **Corpus**: High-quality human essays written and scored in 2021 (strictly before the release of ChatGPT), paired with expert pedagogical critiques.
- **Experimental Prompt**: Models instructed to incorporate teacher feedback and fix grammatical issues *without altering semantic meaning*.
- **Evaluation**: Algorithmic and human evaluation of proposition retention, stance alteration, and semantic cosine drift.

### 3.3 Large-Scale Conference Peer Review Audit
- **Substrate**: Thousands of peer reviews from a recent top-tier AI conference.
- **Identification**: Statistical perplexity and excess-token estimators identifying AI-generated review corpora (~21% prevalence).
- **Comparative Metrics**: Reviewer criteria weighting (clarity, novelty, significance, correctness) and overall recommendation scores.

---

## 4. Key Findings & Quantitative Indicators

1. **+70% Surge in Neutrality**: Extensive LLM assistance increases fence-sitting, non-committal essay outcomes by approximately 70%, proving that models homogenize rhetorical commitment toward the centroid.
2. **Grammar Edits Alter Meaning**: Models asked exclusively to repair grammar systematically alter propositions, indicating that syntactic assistance cannot be decoupled from semantic steering.
3. **Institutional Grade Inflation (+1.0 Point)**: AI-generated peer reviews systematically inflate scores by +1.0 on standard academic rating scales, while deprioritizing scientific clarity and significance.
4. **Erosion of Human Voice**: Writers using LLMs consistently describe a progressive detachment from their own prose, reporting that the text feels generic and alien.
