# Delving into ChatGPT usage in academic writing through excess vocabulary

## 1. Executive Summary
Following the release of ChatGPT in November 2022, Large Language Models rapidly diffused across academia. While detecting AI-written text using traditional classifier models is fraught with false positives and adversarial fragility, tracking population-level vocabulary shifts provides an objective, statistical lens on structural adoption.

Kobak and colleagues analyzed **over 14 million PubMed biomedical abstracts published between 2010 and 2024**. By quantifying anomalous deviations in word frequencies relative to pre-2023 historical baseline trajectories, they identified an abrupt surge in specific style words. The authors estimate that as of 2024, at least **10% to 13.5% of all biomedical abstracts were processed or assisted by LLMs**—with certain non-English-speaking countries and specific disciplines exceeding 30% to 40%. The magnitude of this vocabulary shift eclipses any historical event in academic writing, including the COVID-19 pandemic.

---

## 2. Second-Order Cybernetic Framing

### 2.1 The Infiltration of the Scientific Substrate
In scientific research, academic abstracts form the primary epistemic record and the core training substrate for future scientific AI models. When scientists deploy LLMs to draft or polish manuscripts, they unwittingly introduce statistical attractors into the permanent scientific record:
- **Marker Words as Eigenvalues**: Words like *delves*, *intricate*, *comprehensive*, *underscores*, and *showcasing* act as discrete eigenvalues of the model's policy network.
- **Corpus-Level Feedback**: Because modern LLMs are continuously retrained on new literature (via ongoing web crawls, PubMed dumps, and Semantic Scholar indexes), the excess vocabulary of 2023–2024 forms the ground truth for models trained in 2025–2026. This creates a self-reinforcing, autophagous cycle of scientific prose homogenization.

## 3. Empirical Methodology & Data Substrates

- **Dataset**: All English-language biomedical abstracts indexed in PubMed from 2010 through early 2024 (>14,000,000 documents).
- **Control Group / Baseline**: Temporal counterfactual based on smooth historical trends from 2010 to 2022.
- **Excess Word Metric**: Calculated as:
  $$\text{Excess}(w) = f_{2024}(w) - \hat{f}_{2024}(w)$$
  where $\hat{f}$ is the projected expected frequency from the pre-LLM baseline.
- **Prevalence Lower Bound**: Developed a mixture model lower-bound estimator:
  $$\alpha \ge \max_w \frac{f_{\text{obs}}(w) - \hat{f}(w)}{f_{\text{LLM}}(w) - \hat{f}(w)}$$

---

## 4. Key Findings & Quantitative Indicators

1. **Unprecedented Linguistic Shockwave**: Words like *delves* experienced an increase of over **2,500%** above historical baselines within 18 months of ChatGPT's release.
2. **Global Disparity & Non-Native Amplification**: The excess word frequency was substantially higher in papers authored by researchers in non-English speaking institutions, highlighting that LLMs serve as an equalizer for fluency while simultaneously acting as an engine of lexical homogenization.
3. **Pervasive Baseline Shift**: At least 1 in 8 scientific abstracts in 2024 displayed measurable LLM fingerprinting.
