# The Curse of Recursion: Training on Generated Data Makes Models Forget

## 1. Executive Summary
As generative artificial intelligence scales, models are increasingly trained on text harvested from the web that was itself authored or co-authored by generative models. This foundational paper defines and mathematically proves the phenomenon of **Model Collapse**: a degenerative process whereby successive generations of generative models trained on recursive synthetic data gradually forget rare events, misrepresent distributional tails, and eventually collapse into a single degenerate mode.

The authors show that model collapse occurs across multiple architectures (including Gaussian Mixture Models, Variational Autoencoders, and Large Language Models such as OPT-125m). Over successive generations of recursive retraining, functional variance collapses, culminating in an irreversible loss of informational entropy and cognitive richness.

---

## 2. Second-Order Cybernetic Framing

### 2.1 The Autophagous Degeneration of Closed Systems
In cybernetics, a system that feeds exclusively on its own outputs without replenishing its informational entropy from a rich external environment is subject to entropic decay. Shumailov et al. provide the formal mathematical foundation for why Second-Order Cybernetic loops become pathogenic when human expressive variance is extinguished:
- **First Generation ($n=0$)**: Model is trained on rich, heterogeneous, tail-heavy human linguistic distributions.
- **Intermediate Generations ($n=1 \dots k$)**: Minor statistical sampling errors, finite sample size effects, and functional approximation errors compound. Tail phenomena (heterodox ideas, rare dialectal phrases, nuanced philosophical arguments) are pruned because their probability mass is rounded toward zero.
- **Late Stage (Late Model Collapse)**: The model's output distribution converges onto a singular, low-entropy attractor (**eigenform**), reproducing repetitive cliches with zero distributional variance.

## 3. Mathematical Foundations

The paper models recursion through three accumulating sources of error:
1. **Statistical Approximation Error**: Due to finite sample size, the empirical distribution of synthetic data differs from the true generator distribution.
2. **Functional Approximation Error**: Neural network expressiveness limits prevent exact parameter representation of the true distribution.
3. **Functional Expressive Error**: Unbounded support distributions cannot be fully preserved by bounded parameter regimes.

Let $p_n(x)$ be the probability density at generation $n$. The authors demonstrate that under recursive training:
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \mathcal{D}_{\text{KL}}(p_0 \parallel p_n) = \infty$$
and the variance $\sigma_n^2 \to 0$ as probability mass concentrates irreversibly at the mode centroid.

## 4. Key Findings & Quantitative Indicators

1. **Universality of Collapse**: Early and late model collapse occur regardless of model scale or architecture when uncurated synthetic data exceeds critical contamination thresholds.
2. **Permanent Information Erasure**: Once distributional tails are erased in generation $n$, they cannot be recovered by subsequent generations without re-introducing pristine, un-contaminated historical human data.
3. **The Preservation of the Human Substrate**: Highlights the existential imperative of preserving authentic, idiosyncratic human cultural expression as an irreproducible, non-renewable resource for intelligence.
