# Empirical evidence of Large Language Model's influence on human spoken communication

## 1. Executive Summary
From the printing press to social media, communication technologies have continually reshaped human culture. However, world-scale conversational agents (e.g., ChatGPT) constitute a fundamentally new medium: they encode statistical linguistic patterns within their neural representations and disseminate them across hundreds of millions of daily conversational turns. 

This paper provides the first macro-scale empirical proof that human beings internalize the idiosyncratic lexical choices of LLMs and spontaneously transmit them into unscripted verbal communication. Analyzing **737,083 hours of spontaneous speech across 824,634 podcast episodes**, the authors apply synthetic-control causal inference to demonstrate an abrupt, unprecedented surge in ChatGPT-skewed words (such as *delve*, *showcase*, *boast*, *intricacies*, and *meticulous*) immediately following the public release of ChatGPT in late 2022.

A preregistered laboratory experiment ($N = 496$) confirms that brief, unguided interaction with an LLM leads human participants to adopt its vocabulary as their own. Crucially, this lexical entrenchment persists past cognitive distractor tasks and is verified in forced-choice vocabulary tasks, demonstrating structural entrenchment in the human active lexicon.

## 2. Second-Order Cybernetic Framing

### 2.1 Circular Causality & Observer Contamination
In classical (first-order) natural language processing, LLMs are trained on human-authored corpora; human language is treated as the independent environment from which the model samples. Brinkmann, Yakura, Rahwan et al. demonstrate that this relationship has become circular:
- **Phase A**: LLMs are trained on historical human cultural data.
- **Phase B**: LLMs introduce idiosyncratic probabilistic distortions (e.g., token-sampling preferences and RLHF reward-model artifacts favoring specific vocabulary).
- **Phase C**: Human speakers interact with the models, subconsciously internalize these skewed distributions, and adopt them into their own verbal repertoire.
- **Phase D**: Human-generated spoken and written discourse—now bearing machine-induced lexical signatures—flows back into the public communicative sphere, altering the baseline data distribution for future training rounds.

### 2.2 Structural Coupling in Consensual Domains
Following Maturana and Varela, language operates as a consensual domain of recursive coordination. The human cognitive system structurally couples to the language model. Because humans exhibit cognitive linguistic accommodation (Pickering & Garrod's Interactive Alignment Model), the machine does not simply act as an informational retrieval mechanism; it acts as a **dialectal attractor**, pulling the human consensual domain toward its own statistical centroid.

## 3. Empirical Methodology & Data Substrates

### 3.1 Macro-Scale Observational Study (Podcasts)
- **Corpus Scale**: 824,634 podcast episodes spanning 2018 through 2024 (over 737,000 hours of audio).
- **Screening**: Filtered strictly for unscripted, spontaneous conversational discourse (removing scripted news broadcasts and audiobooks).
- **Statistical Identification**: Synthetic-control methods constructing counterfactual trajectories based on pre-November 2022 vocabulary baselines.
- **Target Vocabulary**: Analysis of tokens identified by prior academic work as disproportionately elevated in ChatGPT generation (e.g., *delve*, *showcase*, *boast*, *intricacies*, *meticulous*, *pivotal*, *realm*).

### 3.2 Controlled Laboratory Experiment ($N = 496$)
- **Design**: Pre-registered randomized trial comparing participants interacting with ChatGPT against control groups performing identical tasks without AI or with static search engines.
- **Distractor Task**: Enforced delay and unrelated cognitive puzzles between AI interaction and verbal production.
- **Measurement**: Free-form verbal description task and forced-choice lexical preference test.

---

## 4. Key Findings & Quantitative Indicators

1. **Abrupt Macro-Scale Lexical Break**: An immediate, statistically significant inflection point occurs precisely in December 2022 across hundreds of thousands of podcast speakers worldwide.
2. **Persistent Entrenchment**: In laboratory conditions, participants exposed to an LLM using target tokens were significantly more likely to utilize those exact tokens in subsequent unassisted tasks ($p < 0.001$), confirming that the lexical shift is not merely temporary imitation but cognitive entrenchment.
3. **Cross-Modal Spillover**: Despite LLMs operating predominantly in written text, the lexical modifications transferred seamlessly into spontaneous verbal spoken speech, indicating deep linguistic internalization.
